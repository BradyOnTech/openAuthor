# The Moving Map

Mara unrolled the map beside the lamp. A red line crossed streets the city had forgotten, pausing at the names of people she had buried. She locked the door. Her brother Ivo had once called that her answer to every difficult question: draw a boundary and trust it to hold.

The brass case lay empty on the table. Ivo had made it before the accident that blinded his right eye. He had stamped a small ferry into its lid so that Mara would remember who had taught her to find a route home.

At midnight, the line reached her own door. The clock struck eleven. In the silence before its next stroke, a woman on the landing asked Mara to return an hour she had never known she possessed.

# The Clocktower

The clocktower had no hands. Mara climbed its narrow stairs with the map shut in the brass case. At the top, the keeper was washing a child's cup. He set it down when she mentioned the missing hour.

"An hour cannot be stolen," he said. "It can only be borrowed from somebody who expects it back."

Mara wanted a name. The keeper gave her an address on a street she had erased from the city's official survey. Years ago, she had signed that correction without walking the street. Nothing about the empty space had seemed worth arguing over.

On her descent, the stairs took longer than they had on the way up. She counted them twice. There were eleven more steps, and the lowest one was wet with river water.

# The Keeper's Ledger

Ivo read the ledger with his good left eye close to the page. Names filled the first column. Debts filled the second. In the third, the keeper had entered a route home for everyone except himself.

Mara found her signature beside a missing street. Beneath it, another hand had written the names of seven families. Their houses had disappeared when she removed the street from the survey. The map was not showing her a possible city. It was returning the part she had helped conceal.

"We can burn it," Ivo said, touching the brass case. "You can still go home."

She closed the ledger instead. At this point she knew the families' names, but not the keeper's. That absence troubled her more than a confession would have. She copied the seven routes onto the lining of her coat before giving the book back.

# The Ferry Debt

The ferryman would not accept money. He wanted the hour Ivo had lost on the day of his accident. Mara said an injury was not a debt. The ferryman shrugged and pushed the boat away from the landing.

Ivo caught the rope before it tightened. He did not look at Mara. "You cannot correct a bargain by pretending I never made it."

They crossed in silence. Mara watched the missing streets appear in the river's reflection. In one of them, a girl was waiting outside a shuttered bakery, old enough now to know she had been promised something.

On the far bank Ivo asked for the map. Mara kept the brass case, but gave him the copy sewn into her coat. It was the first route she had trusted him to choose since the accident.

# The Blank Hour

Inside the missing street, nothing moved until Mara spoke one of the names from the ledger. Then a shutter opened. Then a door. The girl outside the bakery turned and called for her father.

The keeper arrived without his cup. He told Mara the city could hold its lost streets or its stolen hour, but not both. Close the last door before dawn and the families could return. Leave it open and the missing hour would come back to everyone who had paid for it.

Mara asked his name. For the first time, he looked frightened. "You have enough names to carry," he said.

She sent Ivo ahead with the seven routes. His task was to guide the families to the last door, not to decide what Mara would do there. The brass case was still in her hands when the bakery clock began to tick.

# The Last Door

At the last door, Mara counted seven families. Ivo stood beside the ferryman, his good left eye on the river. He had brought everyone to the threshold without losing a single name.

The keeper waited on the other side. Mara could not see the city behind him. She could see only the child's cup, balanced on a windowsill, and the blank space where her own street ought to have been.

Mara closed the last door before dawn. The seven families were safe. She put the map in the brass case and followed Ivo home.

At breakfast, neither of them mentioned the keeper. Mara drew a new survey in which every street had a name. She left the space at the bottom of the page empty.
