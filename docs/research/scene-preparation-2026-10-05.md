# Preparing fiction scenes before drafting

Checked October 5, 2026. A recent openAuthor chapter changed the meaning of an invented timestamp partway through the scene and gave an insufficient account of the duration behind a spoiled shipment. The next experiment tests preparation of consequential actions and states through the existing plan-then-draft workflow. Documentation supports this mechanism as a useful approach to investigate; it does not establish its literary benefit.

**Current public workflows**

Sudowrite describes Scenes as moments generally sharing a location, timeframe or character context. Draft uses the supplied Scenes alongside selected story and style information. Before prose generation, it offers suggestions when a scene is too short, sprawling or unclear; authors can accept or ignore them. This is preparation that a writer can inspect, rather than a guarantee that the resulting chapter is sound. [Scenes & Draft](https://docs.sudowrite.com/using-sudowrite/1ow1qkGqof9rtcyGnrWUBS/scenes--chapter-prose/49p5MTVxTKkVFEC5rVUzpY).

Novelcrafter distinguishes a scene summary from a smaller beat used to generate a moment of prose. Prior scene summaries also supply context. Its examples connect a goal to conflict, outcome, reaction, dilemma and decision. Those examples offer a causal organization; quiet scenes need not be forced through that sequence. [Summaries and beats](https://www.novelcrafter.com/help/faq/plan/where-do-i-put-my-scene-chapter-act-book-summary-what-about-my-beats).

Sudowrite's guidance also identifies conflicting or ambiguous story information as a cause of unsuccessful prose. Its continuity example shows why a vague reference to what a character did needs the relevant earlier event supplied. The useful inference for our test is to make consequential state explicit without replacing uncertainty or requiring exhaustive logistics. [Tips & Tricks](https://docs.sudowrite.com/using-sudowrite/1ow1qkGqof9rtcyGnrWUBS/tips--tricks/eBjBne7foMi8uYFxWEPCai).

**The openAuthor experiment**

Use the existing Change Set plan stage for a new scene. Route an empty manuscript to scene preparation rather than editing existing prose. The craft reference asks the planner for causal movements and the consequential states later movements depend on: time relationships, access, possession, conditional promises and unresolved knowledge. Permitted local inventions remain proposals rather than established history.

Freeze the same QA brief, permissions, Memory selection, original direction, model and medium effort. Preserve the first plan and the first resulting chapter. The plan call has a 1,800-token target; the prose call retains the 4,400-token target and authored 1,800–2,200-word range. Both are existing ChatGPT-backed inference; no additional account is used. Read the entire result against the earlier direct draft and retain failures. No generated chapter is automatically applied.

This is an adaptive workflow comparison after inspecting the direct draft. It adds a plan call and changes planning guidance, so any improvement cannot be attributed to the skill alone or described as equal-budget superiority. It supplies no independent reader preference or native competing-product output. A transfer scene must also check whether planning adds useful specificity without imposing a formula on quieter fiction.

**Observed outcome**

The actual chapter and quiet-scene comparison (withheld from the public repo because it quotes a private manuscript) did not establish a clear literary gain. The technical chapter was still over length; both quiet drafts met their local requirements with different strengths. The added preparation paragraph was removed from the shipped kit. The accurate empty-scene planning role remains. No automatic planning call or universal narrative template was added.
