## Read the chapter first

### The delivery

Nessa brought the letter flat against her chest, under the coat that had belonged to her brother. The coat was too broad across the shoulders. When she lifted her hand to knock, a little rain ran from the cuff onto the step. She put her hand back over the envelope.

Ivo opened the workshop door without taking the chain off. Behind him, a lamp stood on a box of hinges. The yellow shade had been mended with thread. Nessa could see two of the stitches through the narrow opening.

"For you," she said.

"A generous description of most trouble."

"From the harbor office."

He unhooked the chain. She went in and laid the letter on the table. The green wax seal was whole. Her brother had asked her to deliver it before the second bell; he had not told her what it said. She had not asked him to.

Ivo moved a bowl of screws away from the wet patch under her sleeve. He did not touch the letter. A ruler lay across a sheet covered with printed numbers. Beside the ruler someone had drawn a red line in pencil. The line ended well short of the edge.

"Is that where the water comes?" Nessa asked.

"That's where they propose to put a mooring. The water hasn't agreed."

He turned the sheet so she could read its title. Tide Table, in small black capitals. She read the nearest column twice. The numbers had the plainness of things already settled, although she could not tell which column belonged to today.

"Will the boat go?"

"There's a man paid to decide."

"Are you him?"

"An economical appointment, but no."

On the wall, the clock showed twenty past two. Its second hand stayed between the same pair of marks. Ivo followed her glance and said it had stopped on Tuesday. Outside, someone dragged a chain across the stones. Nessa could not see the quay from the workshop.

He folded the tide table along its old crease and gave it to her. She held it by the dry corner. The letter remained on the table with its seal facing up. Ivo had neither opened it nor agreed to carry it anywhere.


### The waiting room

The waiting room had three chairs and a bench long enough for four people who knew one another. Nessa took a chair. Its back pressed a strip of cold wood between her shoulders. She moved the coat's collar under it and unfolded the tide table on her knees.

A woman behind the counter was tying a packet with string. Each time she pulled the knot tight, the packet turned a little. Nessa watched the corners travel around the same stain on the counter.

"Has anyone decided about the boat?"

"You'll hear the bell."

"There was one already."

The woman took her fingers out of the string. "You'll hear another."

Nessa looked down at the sheet. She had brought it because Ivo had given it to her, not because she had found a use for it. Without his ruler, she could not be sure she was following a row straight across. She put a fingernail under one number and held it there.

A boy came through the outside door with a bucket. He set it under the leak beside the bench and went away again. No one asked him about the boat. The first drop hit the bucket's rim, the next its bottom. Nessa shifted her chair half an inch so the falling water was out of her line of sight.

The woman put the tied packet on a shelf and took down an empty one. She did not open the shutters. Beyond them Nessa could hear a cart, then two men calling to each other. Their words did not reach the room. Nothing she heard told her the current time or the height of the water.

She folded the tide table. The red mark was on the outside now. She wanted the ruler, although she knew a ruler would not make the office's answer more exact. At the counter she asked whether she could go back to the workshop.

"If you're listening," the woman said.

Nessa held the dry corner of the sheet inside her coat and went out. She listened all the way across the yard. No second bell sounded before she reached Ivo's door.


### The ruler

Ivo was sitting where she had left him, with the bowl of screws beside his elbow. He had moved the letter to a dry square of table. Its green seal was still whole. The workshop clock still showed twenty past two; Nessa knew it had stopped, and did not use it to count how long she had been away.

"The ruler," she said.

He lifted it from the table. "The office misplaced a straight line?"

"I couldn't follow the numbers."

She laid the tide table beside the sealed letter. She felt nervous about what he might do next. She wanted to ask whether he would open the letter, but she did not want to sound as if she were asking him to.

"The red mark," she said. "It's only a mooring?"

"A proposed one. Even the pencil was careful."

The ruler lay across the lower edge of the sheet. Its end rested on the red line. A short length of black thread from Nessa's cuff was caught beneath it. She looked at the thread. Neither of them had opened the letter. Neither had said what it contained.

Ivo reached toward the envelope. Nessa put two fingers on its folded edge.

"Wait," she said.

He left his hand on the table. The ruler stayed where he had laid it, with the black thread caught beneath its end.

## Alternative B

She laid the tide table beside the sealed letter, its green seal intact. The figures could not tell her where the water stood now. The stopped clock could not tell her how long remained before he left—if he meant to leave at all.

She wanted to ask whether he would open the letter. The question felt too much like permission.

“The red mark,” she said. “It’s only a mooring?”

“A proposed one. Even the pencil was careful.”

The ruler lay across the lower edge of the sheet, its end on the red line. A short length of black thread from Nessa’s cuff was caught beneath it. She fixed on the thread: pinned, but not broken. Easier to look at than the green seal.

Neither of them had opened the letter. Neither knew what it contained.

Ivo reached toward the envelope. Nessa put two fingers on its folded edge.

“Wait,” she said.

He left his hand resting on the table. The ruler stayed where he had laid it, the black thread caught beneath its end.
