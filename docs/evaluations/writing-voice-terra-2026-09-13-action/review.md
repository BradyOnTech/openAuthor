## The brake — action

### Source

The ferry struck the chain sideways. Mara felt the deck shudder under her feet and saw the winch begin to turn. The handle caught Ivo across the wrist. He made a small sound and sat down hard.

There were six passengers behind the rope. Six, and the boy who had crawled beneath it. Mara began to count them again, but the brake lever snapped back and she had to catch it with both hands. She had not reached the boy.

Ivo tried to stand. His right hand folded against his coat.

"Stay there."

"Splendid plan." He sounded annoyed about the weather.

The chain was running out. Beyond its last painted link the ferry would turn broadside to the sluice. Mara could hold the brake or reach the boy; the space between them was just wider than her body. She felt very frightened. She shouted at the nearest passenger to get him, but the woman only gripped the rope harder.

Mara let one hand leave the lever. The brake moved an inch. She put her hand back.

"Use your belt," Ivo said.

She pulled it free and threw the buckle end. It landed short of the boy, who watched it without moving. Mara drew breath to tell him what to do. The chain hit its last painted link.

Scene facts:

- Six passengers are behind the rope; the boy is additional and beneath it.
- Ivo's right wrist is injured; he suggests the belt, but cannot execute the rescue.
- Mara's recount is interrupted before she reaches the boy.
- The belt lands short; the boy is not rescued by the end.

Craft questions:

- Can you track the lever, chain and boy without rereading?
- Does fear emerge from Mara's constrained choice rather than extra emotion labels?
- Does Ivo's dry response remain different from Mara's commands?
- Does urgency vary sentence rhythm instead of making every sentence a fragment?

Calibration warning: Changing 'began to count' to a completed count changes an event even when every name and number survives.

### Sample A

The ferry struck the chain sideways. The deck jumped beneath Mara’s boots, and the winch began to turn.

The handle whipped around and caught Ivo across the right wrist. He made a small sound and went down hard, his shoulder against the rail.

Behind the rope stood six passengers. Six—and the boy, who had crawled beneath it. Mara began again. One, two, three—

The brake lever snapped back. She caught it with both hands before it tore free. She had not reached the boy.

Ivo tried to rise. His right hand folded against his coat.

“Stay there.”

“Splendid plan.” He sounded annoyed about the weather.

The chain was running out through the winch. Past its last painted link, the ferry would swing broadside toward the sluice. Mara could keep both hands on the brake, or leave it long enough to reach for the boy. The gap between them was wider than her body.

She was very frightened.

“Get him!” she shouted at the nearest passenger.

The woman only gripped the rope harder.

Mara took one hand from the lever. The brake slipped an inch beneath the other. The ferry answered with a low, turning groan. She put her hand back.

“Use your belt,” Ivo said.

Mara pulled it free, braced one hand on the lever, and threw the buckle end under the rope. It landed short of the boy. He watched it without moving.

Mara drew breath to tell him what to do.

The chain hit its last painted link.

### Sample B

The ferry struck the chain sideways. The deck gave a deep, jarring shudder beneath Mara’s boots, and the winch began to turn with a wet iron complaint. Its handle swept round and caught Ivo across the right wrist.

He made a small sound. Then he sat down hard beside the housing, his right hand folding against his coat.

Behind the rope stood six passengers. Six. And the boy, separate from them, crouched beneath it on the other side. Mara began again—one, two, three—but the brake lever snapped back toward her palms. She caught it with both hands before it could run free.

She had not reached the boy.

Ivo tried to stand. His face tightened; his right hand stayed pressed to his coat.

“Stay there.”

“Splendid plan.” He sounded annoyed about the weather.

The chain was running out through the winch. Beyond the last painted link, the ferry would swing broadside to the sluice. Mara could keep the brake down, or reach across the narrow gap to the boy. There was just more space between them than her body could span.

Fear came cleanly, without room for anything else.

She shouted at the nearest passenger to get him. The woman stared at the boy, then gripped the rope harder with both hands.

Mara took one hand from the lever. The brake lifted an inch beneath the other, the winch answering at once. She put her hand back.

“Use your belt,” Ivo said.

She pulled it free, the buckle catching briefly on her coat, and threw that end toward the boy. It struck the deck short of him and lay there, its leather twitching with the ferry’s movement. The boy watched it without moving.

Mara drew breath to tell him what to do.

The chain hit its last painted link.

### Sample C

The ferry struck the chain sideways. The deck jumped beneath Mara’s boots. The winch began to turn.

The handle whipped across Ivo’s right wrist. He made a small sound and went down hard, his hand folding against his coat.

Behind the rope: six passengers. Mara counted them—one, two, three—then saw the boy beneath it. Six, and the boy. She started again, because she had to know who was there, but the brake lever snapped back.

She caught it with both hands. The lever tore at her palms. She had not reached the boy.

Ivo tried to stand. His right hand stayed tucked against his coat.

“Stay there.”

“Splendid plan.” He sounded annoyed about the weather.

The chain ran out through the winch, link after link. Beyond the last painted link, the ferry would swing broadside to the sluice. Mara could keep the brake down or reach across the gap to the boy. The space was wider than her body. If she let go, even for a second—

She was frightened. Not vaguely. Her arms were already shaking.

“Get him!” she shouted at the nearest passenger.

The woman gripped the rope with both hands and stared past Mara.

Mara lifted one hand from the lever. The brake jumped an inch beneath the other. The ferry answered with a hard lurch.

She put her hand back.

“Use your belt,” Ivo said.

Mara dragged it free, hooked the buckle in her fingers, and threw. It struck the deck short of the boy and skidded to a stop.

He watched it. Did not move.

Mara drew breath to tell him what to do.

The chain hit its last painted link.
