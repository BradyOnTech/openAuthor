# Local Memory statement filtering — October 2, 2026

The local Memory scan previously proposed conditional, quoted, imagined and reported claims as explicit facts or connections. For example, `If Elin trusts Tomas, he will answer` produced a trust connection; `Tomas imagined that Elin was a spy` produced a spy fact. These suggestions were provisional, but their high confidence and declarative explanation still added misleading review work.

The scanner now excludes quoted spans while retaining original Unicode offsets. A narrow clause guard withholds recognizable conditional, speculative, attributed and reported claims. Question punctuation blocks the corresponding claim. Semicolons end fact values and isolate unrelated clauses. Direct declarations, negative values and explicit distrust remain reviewable; a stated belief remains a belief about its subject rather than a separate fact about its object. Exact unambiguous accepted names and aliases inside the claim are removed from the uncertainty-check view, so names such as `Maybe` survive as subjects, connection targets and possessive values. Preceding conditional/reporting words remain visible. An indirect complement ending in `that` before the embedded subject is withheld without expanding a list of literary reporting verbs.

This changes derived suggestions only. Scans do not edit manuscript prose, approved identities, Canon Facts or connections. An unreviewed claim can retire when its source becomes hypothetical; previously accepted facts, author corrections and review decisions remain author-owned. The optional reviewed semantic scan continues to preserve its evidence and unresolved type confirmation.

## Remaining limits

These authored cases establish the recorded behavior, not novel-scale accuracy or observed author review effort. The guard recognizes a limited set of English uncertainty/reporting markers and common quote forms; it is not a grammatical or semantic parser. It can withhold valid statements with ambiguous quotation or reported phrasing and can miss other ways to express uncertainty. The indirect-complement rule deliberately withholds even affirmative reports such as `verified that`; it does not evaluate the reporting verb's truth implications. Indirect claims without a recognized marker or complement boundary can still pass. Direct narration and exact evidence are not proof of truth. Extracting arbitrary uncased names and deciding whether an ambiguous name denotes a person, place, ship or title still require author review or a separately evaluated semantic interpretation. A fresh manuscript and observed author triage are needed before claiming the Memory-accuracy priority complete.

A further root probe reproduced an escaped nested quotation turning a reported trust claim into a connection. The [red record](escaped-quote-red.json) remains inspectable; quote masking now keeps odd-backslash escaped delimiters inside the enclosing quote and preserves the direct statement after it. The [scale sample](current-scale.json) measured three full scans of a disposable 400-scene/72-entity book at 4.56–4.83 seconds before this quote refinement; its exact historical source hash is retained. It is a synthetic latency observation, not observed author review effort.

Independent Spec review also reproduced present-tense suffix attribution (says, believes and reports) still producing explicit suggestions. Prefix and comma-suffix handling now share the same supported reporting-marker vocabulary. All three original probes are included in the authored cases; the reviewer verified the repair and reports zero remaining actionable findings in this iteration.
