# Pressure and duration

Trace changes in pressure, choices and information. Repeated beats that leave all three unchanged may stall the scene, but a deliberate wait or recurring action can carry dread, grief or ritual. Judge the effect, not the sentence count.

Spend prose on the consequential moment: the difficult choice, the uncertain response, the perception that changes a tactic. Compress routine transitions when they dilute that moment, while retaining spatial orientation and the setup that makes the consequence legible.

Increase tension through a specific constraint, incompatible aim, withheld answer or cost already supported by the story. Do not manufacture danger, certainty, melodrama or a new antagonist to satisfy a request for urgency. A technical failure should change what the characters can do, not merely become another ominous sound.

Vary the duration and rhythm across an exchange. Leave breathing room where contrast makes pressure felt. Preserve the stopping point: an unresolved action is not an invitation to finish it, and every chapter need not end with an interchangeable warning or rhetorical flourish.
