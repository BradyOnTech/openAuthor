# Speech and tactics

For each speaker, identify what they want from the exchange, what they can know and what they withhold. Their speech is an attempt: persuade, evade, bargain, test, comfort, expose, terminate or refuse. A response should affect the next tactic when the scene depends on conflict.

Distinguish voices through vocabulary, syntax, specificity, omissions and conversational habits grounded in supplied character evidence. Do not give every speaker the narrator's wit, lyricism or technical expertise. Distinctness is not a collection of repeated catchphrases or accents.

Use subtext where the situation benefits from it; direct speech, blunt demands and practical explanations may be right. Avoid making characters explain shared knowledge for the reader's benefit. A technical exchange gains drama when knowledge changes leverage, access or trust.

Actions between lines should matter to the exchange or its perception. Repeated cup handling, nodding and glancing add motion without necessarily adding meaning. A fast untagged exchange or a meaningful silence can carry pressure. Maintain attribution clarity, physical continuity and the original emotional turn. Do not append a confession or response past a protected ending.
