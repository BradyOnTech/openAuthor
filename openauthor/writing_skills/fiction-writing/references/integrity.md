# Story integrity

Separate author knowledge, reader knowledge, character belief and confirmed character knowledge. A character may infer, suspect, misremember or lie; preserve the exact subject and degree of uncertainty. Read chronology from supplied time labels and source evidence, not merely chapter order. Honor the active Storyline's changes and protected constraints.

For revision, compare events and physical states, not only names and keywords. An interrupted reach must remain interrupted; giving an object differs from its current possession; an unsealed letter differs from an unopened one. Retaining a sentence elsewhere does not repair a contradiction introduced nearby.

The scene contract describes the intended purpose, turn and stopping point. Follow it within the author's explicit permissions. Background lore constrains a revision; it is not a shopping list of facts to insert. Do not add a discovery, confession, completed action or resolved uncertainty merely to finish a polished paragraph. A new scene may develop the authored beats, while an existing passage's endpoint remains authoritative unless the author requests a change.

Preserve the causal facts, knowledge limits, protected wording and successful details established in the source. If instruction and evidence conflict, flag that conflict in a plan or critique; do not covertly change facts to make the instruction easier.
