# Narrative promises

Identify the existing promise, clue, recurring detail or withheld question and the later consequence it supports. Distinguish an authored connection from a new suggested connection. Keep reader knowledge separate from character knowledge.

A useful setup affects present perception or action while preparing a later interpretation. Avoid inserting a conspicuous explanation of what the clue will mean. An echo may change meaning as the character changes; repeating the same phrase without consequence is not automatically a payoff.

Respect which mysteries are intentionally unresolved and which revelations belong later. Do not supply a convenient origin, conspiracy explanation or resolution because it would make the current scene feel complete. Relevant distant evidence can constrain a local scene without being restated in it.

When the necessary distant passage is unavailable, say what would be needed to judge the connection in a plan or critique. Do not certify a whole-book payoff from nearby scenes alone. A revision should preserve existing setup unless the author's scope explicitly changes that thread.
