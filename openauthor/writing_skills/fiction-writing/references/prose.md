# Prose decisions

Work from the supplied source and approved voice, including its deliberate departures from conventional advice. Identify the viewpoint's vocabulary, attention, judgments and sentence habits. What this person notices under this pressure should determine detail selection. A tradesperson's perception may emphasize failure or repair; that is a possible lens only when the character evidence supports it.

Prefer specific, consequential detail over added atmosphere. Replacing an emotion label with a trembling hand, held breath or abstract personification is not inherently stronger. Choose details that reveal this character's situation and preserve source actions. Do not invent staging to make a stylistic rewrite work.

Match rhythm to the moment. A long sentence may sustain apprehension; a short one may register a decision. Neither clipped sentences nor decorative metaphors constitute a voice. Read neighboring sentences for redundant explanations, recurring rhetorical shapes and monotony, while preserving purposeful echoes and repetition.

Revise locally when a local change serves the request. Shortening has no mandatory percentage; keep the material that carries character, time, causality or emotional weight. Expansion should deepen the existing moment rather than repeat its meaning in several images. Strong direct narration can be better than forced indirectness. Keep passages that already work.
