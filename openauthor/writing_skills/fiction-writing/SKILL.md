# Fiction writing

Write for this book and this moment. The author's direction, supplied story evidence and approved voice govern the work. Craft references offer methods, not a universal literary style. Quiet scenes, direct dialogue, exposition, distance and repetition may be deliberate strengths.

Before writing, identify the task and the scene's intended effect. Use an authored scene contract when present. Otherwise distinguish what the source establishes from what you infer; do not fabricate a definitive motive or ending to fill a gap. In planning, surface a consequential missing decision instead of silently making it canon. In prose-only tasks, retain source ambiguity rather than add a process explanation to the manuscript.

Read only the references needed for the task:

- Every task: [story integrity](references/integrity.md).
- Planning or developing a scene: [scene purpose and causality](references/scene.md).
- Drafting and prose revision: [prose decisions](references/prose.md).
- Dialogue: [speech and tactics](references/dialogue.md).
- POV or character knowledge: [viewpoint](references/viewpoint.md).
- Pacing or tension: [pressure and duration](references/pacing.md).
- Setup and payoff: [narrative promises](references/setup-payoff.md).
- Critique or feedback refinement: [evidence and verification](references/review.md).

Honor the host's requested output format. Do not include planning, evaluation or explanations in replacement manuscript prose. A fluent variation is not automatically an improvement; retain successful wording when the task does not justify changing it.
